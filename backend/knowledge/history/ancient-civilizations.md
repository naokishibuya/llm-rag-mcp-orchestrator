# Ancient Civilizations

## Mesopotamia (c. 3500-539 BC)

The "Cradle of Civilization" between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers (modern Iraq). The Sumerians invented cuneiform writing around 3400 BC — the earliest known writing system. They also invented the wheel, the plow, and the 60-minute hour. The Code of Hammurabi (c. 1754 BC) was one of the earliest written legal codes, with 282 laws carved on a stone pillar.

## Ancient Egypt (c. 3100-30 BC)

Egyptian civilization lasted over 3,000 years along the Nile River. The Great Pyramid of Giza was built around 2560 BC and remained the tallest structure in the world for nearly 4,000 years. It contains approximately 2.3 million stone blocks averaging 2.5 tons each. Cleopatra VII lived closer in time to the Moon landing (1969) than to the building of the Great Pyramid. The ancient Egyptians invented papyrus paper, surgical instruments, and toothpaste (made from crushed rock, mint, and dried flowers).

## Ancient Greece (c. 800-146 BC)

Greece gave us democracy, philosophy, theater, and the Olympic Games (first held in 776 BC). Athens had a direct democracy where citizens voted on laws — though only free adult males could participate (about 10-20% of the population). Greek philosophers Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle laid the foundations of Western philosophy. The Library of Alexandria, founded around 283 BC, was the ancient world's greatest center of learning, said to contain hundreds of thousands of scrolls.

## Roman Empire (27 BC - 476 AD)

At its peak, the Roman Empire spanned 5 million square kilometers and governed about 70 million people (roughly 25% of the world's population). The Romans built 400,000 km of roads, of which 80,000 km were paved stone — many still exist today. Roman concrete (opus caementicium) was so durable that structures like the Pantheon have stood for nearly 2,000 years. The Colosseum could seat 50,000 spectators and had a retractable awning system.

## Ancient China

China's civilization stretches back to the Shang Dynasty (c. 1600 BC). The Four Great Inventions — paper, printing, gunpowder, and the compass — transformed world history. The Great Wall, built and rebuilt over centuries, stretches about 21,196 km. Emperor Qin Shi Huang unified China in 221 BC and was buried with the Terracotta Army: over 8,000 life-sized clay soldiers, each with unique facial features.
