# Key Moments in Modern History

## The Industrial Revolution (1760-1840)

Beginning in Britain, the Industrial Revolution transformed society from agrarian to industrial. James Watt's improved steam engine (1769) powered factories, trains, and ships. By 1850, Britain produced half the world's iron and cotton cloth. The revolution brought railways, telegraph communication, and mass production — but also child labor, pollution, and urban overcrowding.

## The Age of Revolutions

- **American Revolution (1775-1783)**: Thirteen colonies declared independence from Britain. The Declaration of Independence (1776) stated "all men are created equal" — though this initially applied only to white male property owners.
- **French Revolution (1789-1799)**: Overthrew the monarchy, leading to the Declaration of the Rights of Man. The revolutionary motto "Liberty, Equality, Fraternity" influenced democratic movements worldwide.

## World War I (1914-1918)

Triggered by the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand, WWI involved over 30 countries and caused approximately 20 million deaths. The war introduced tanks, chemical weapons, and aerial combat. It reshaped the map of Europe: four empires (Ottoman, Austro-Hungarian, Russian, German) collapsed.

## World War II (1939-1945)

The deadliest conflict in human history with an estimated 70-85 million deaths. D-Day (June 6, 1944) was the largest amphibious invasion ever, with 156,000 Allied troops landing in Normandy. The war ended with the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki. It led to the creation of the United Nations (1945) and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948).

## The Cold War (1947-1991)

A geopolitical rivalry between the United States and Soviet Union that never escalated to direct military conflict between the two powers. It drove the Space Race, the nuclear arms race, and proxy wars in Korea, Vietnam, and Afghanistan. The fall of the Berlin Wall on November 9, 1989 symbolized the end of the Cold War, followed by the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991.

## The Digital Revolution

- **1969**: ARPANET, the precursor to the internet, sent its first message.
- **1983**: The internet adopted TCP/IP, becoming the network we know today.
- **1991**: Tim Berners-Lee launched the World Wide Web.
- **2007**: The iPhone launched, beginning the smartphone era.
- **2022-present**: Large Language Models like ChatGPT brought AI into mainstream daily use.
