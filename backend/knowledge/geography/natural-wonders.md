# Natural Wonders of the World

## Mountains

- **Mount Everest** (8,849 m): The tallest mountain above sea level, located on the border of Nepal and Tibet. Over 6,000 people have reached the summit since Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay first did it in 1953. The summit is so high that jet stream winds can exceed 320 km/h.
- **K2** (8,611 m): The second tallest but far more dangerous than Everest. Known as the "Savage Mountain," it has a fatality rate of about 25% for those who attempt the summit.
- **Mount Kilimanjaro** (5,895 m): Africa's highest peak and the tallest freestanding mountain in the world. Located in Tanzania, it has five distinct climate zones from base to summit, ranging from tropical to arctic.

## Canyons and Gorges

- **Grand Canyon** (USA): Up to 1,800 m deep and 29 km wide, carved by the Colorado River over 5-6 million years. The exposed rock layers span nearly 2 billion years of Earth's geological history.
- **Yarlung Tsangpo Grand Canyon** (Tibet): The deepest canyon in the world at 6,009 m, even deeper than the Grand Canyon.

## Water Features

- **Victoria Falls** (Zambia/Zimbabwe): Known locally as "Mosi-oa-Tunya" (The Smoke That Thunders). At 1,708 m wide and 108 m tall, it creates the largest curtain of falling water on Earth. The spray can be seen from 50 km away.
- **Great Barrier Reef** (Australia): The largest living structure on Earth, stretching 2,300 km. It's visible from space and contains over 1,500 species of fish, 400 types of coral, and 4,000 species of mollusk.
- **Amazon River**: Carries more water than the next seven largest rivers combined. The Amazon basin contains about 20% of the world's fresh water.

## Deserts and Extremes

- **Sahara Desert**: The world's largest hot desert at 9.2 million sq km — roughly the size of the United States. Temperatures can exceed 50C during the day and drop below freezing at night.
- **Mariana Trench**: The deepest point in the ocean at 10,994 m below sea level. The water pressure at the bottom is over 1,000 times atmospheric pressure at sea level. Only three crewed expeditions have reached the bottom.
- **Dead Sea**: At 430 m below sea level, it is the lowest point on Earth's surface. Its water is nearly 10 times saltier than the ocean, making it easy to float.
