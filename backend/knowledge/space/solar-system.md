# The Solar System

Our solar system consists of the Sun and everything gravitationally bound to it, including 8 planets, 5 recognized dwarf planets, and countless smaller objects.

## The Planets

- **Mercury**: Closest to the Sun. No atmosphere, extreme temperature swings from -180C to 430C. A day on Mercury (sunrise to sunrise) lasts 176 Earth days.
- **Venus**: Hottest planet at 465C due to runaway greenhouse effect. It rotates backwards (retrograde) and a day is longer than its year.
- **Earth**: The only known planet with liquid water on the surface and confirmed life. Its moon is unusually large relative to the planet.
- **Mars**: The Red Planet, colored by iron oxide dust. Home to Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the solar system at 21.9 km high. Mars has two tiny moons: Phobos and Deimos.
- **Jupiter**: Largest planet — 1,300 Earths could fit inside. The Great Red Spot is a storm that has raged for at least 350 years. Jupiter has 95 known moons, including Ganymede, the largest moon in the solar system.
- **Saturn**: Famous for its spectacular ring system made of ice and rock particles. Saturn is less dense than water — it would float in a giant bathtub. Its moon Titan has a thick atmosphere and liquid methane lakes.
- **Uranus**: Tilted 98 degrees on its axis, essentially rolling around the Sun on its side. It was the first planet discovered with a telescope (by William Herschel in 1781).
- **Neptune**: The windiest planet with speeds up to 2,100 km/h. Its moon Triton orbits backwards and is slowly spiraling inward.

## Dwarf Planets

Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006. Other dwarf planets include Eris (slightly more massive than Pluto), Haumea (egg-shaped), Makemake, and Ceres (located in the asteroid belt).

## The Sun

The Sun contains 99.86% of the solar system's total mass. It is a G-type main-sequence star about 4.6 billion years old, roughly halfway through its life. Every second, the Sun converts about 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium through nuclear fusion.
