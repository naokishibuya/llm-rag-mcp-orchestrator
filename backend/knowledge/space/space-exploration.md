# Space Exploration Milestones

## The Space Race (1957-1975)

- **1957**: Sputnik 1 (USSR) became the first artificial satellite to orbit Earth. It was only 58 cm in diameter and beeped for 21 days.
- **1961**: Yuri Gagarin (USSR) became the first human in space aboard Vostok 1, completing one orbit in 108 minutes.
- **1963**: Valentina Tereshkova (USSR) became the first woman in space aboard Vostok 6.
- **1969**: Apollo 11 landed on the Moon. Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin spent 2.5 hours walking on the surface. Armstrong's first step was watched by an estimated 600 million people.
- **1970**: Apollo 13 suffered an oxygen tank explosion en route to the Moon. The crew survived by using the lunar module as a lifeboat — one of NASA's finest hours.
- **1971**: The USSR launched Salyut 1, the first space station.

## The Shuttle Era and Space Stations

- **1981**: The Space Shuttle Columbia made its first flight, ushering in the era of reusable spacecraft.
- **1990**: The Hubble Space Telescope was launched, revolutionizing astronomy. Its initial mirror flaw was corrected in 1993.
- **1998**: Construction of the International Space Station (ISS) began. It has been continuously occupied since November 2000 — the longest continuous human presence in space.

## Modern Exploration

- **2004**: SpaceShipOne won the Ansari X Prize, becoming the first private crewed spacecraft.
- **2012**: NASA's Curiosity rover landed on Mars using a sky crane maneuver.
- **2015**: New Horizons flew past Pluto, revealing a heart-shaped nitrogen glacier.
- **2019**: The Event Horizon Telescope captured the first image of a black hole (in galaxy M87).
- **2020**: SpaceX Crew Dragon became the first commercial spacecraft to carry astronauts to the ISS.
- **2022**: The James Webb Space Telescope began capturing images from the early universe, seeing galaxies formed just 300 million years after the Big Bang.
- **2024**: NASA's Artemis program aims to return humans to the Moon for the first time since 1972.

## Fun Facts

- The ISS orbits Earth every 90 minutes at about 28,000 km/h. Astronauts see 16 sunrises and sunsets per day.
- The Voyager 1 spacecraft, launched in 1977, is the most distant human-made object at over 24 billion km from Earth. It carries a golden record with sounds and images of Earth.
- There are more than 30,000 pieces of tracked space debris orbiting Earth.
